# Brazil Foreign Exchange (FX) Compliance Guidelines

## 1. General Principles
The Brazilian Real (BRL) is a restricted currency. All foreign exchange transactions involving BRL must be registered with the Central Bank of Brazil (Banco Central do Brasil - BCB).

## 2. Inbound Remittances
- **Reporting Requirement:** Any inflow of funds exceeding USD 10,000 (or equivalent) requires an exchange contract (Contrato de Câmbio) linked to a specific underlying transaction type (e.g., capital injection, export revenue, service payment).
- **IOF Tax:** A Tax on Financial Operations (IOF) may apply. The rate varies depending on the nature of the inflow (e.g., intercompany loans may have different rates compared to equity investments).
- **Documentation:** The beneficiary in Brazil must provide supporting documentation (invoice, loan agreement) to the local bank to release funds.

## 3. Outbound Remittances
- **Authorization:** Remittances abroad require proof of tax compliance (e.g., payment of Withholding Income Tax - IRRF).
- **Profit Repatriation:** Dividends and profits can be remitted abroad freely, provided they are supported by the company's financial statements and the foreign investment is registered in the RDE-IED module of the Central Bank system.

## 4. Large Value Transactions
- Transactions exceeding USD 1,000,000 usually require special approval or prior notification to the Treasury department to ensure sufficient liquidity provider availability.
- "Spot" transactions must be settled within 2 business days.

## 5. Restrictions
- Netting of payables and receivables in foreign currency is generally prohibited unless specifically authorized.
- BRL cannot be physically transferred offshore; all conversion happens onshore in Brazil.
